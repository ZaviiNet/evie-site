AI bots

A bot is a model plus a role plus a policy. Bots are what you tag in a room: @skeptic, @advocate.

Two rules that are not options

1. Tagging is enforced by the router, not by the bot's good behaviour. A bot is invoked only when its tag is present, so "only speak when tagged" is a fact about the plumbing rather than a request the model can eventually ignore. A participant that ignores the rule would win the room by volume, and that is the one participant you would keep hearing from.

2. Costs are marked measured or not. Exactly one number on this page came from a real exchange — mine. Everything else is a projection, and a projection resting on an unexamined premise is how a debate gets priced at the wrong order of magnitude. So: measured and not measured are separate states, and the cheap/free columns never wear the clothes of the measured one.

3. Every bot except the agent is text-only. No tools, no memory writes — shown below as a locked field rather than a toggle, because a switch implies it is a decision and it is not. Handing a third-party model shell access is not a feature.

Archive-by-default because a hard delete breaks every past transcript that references the bot — and a transcript whose attribution points at nothing is worse than a long list.

So a room full of free bots has a £0 money cap and a very real latency cap. The cap is on the resource actually being spent.

Mix the tiers by role

Cheap chairs on volume — enumerate options, attack the current position from six angles, hunt the failure case. Volume is where breadth helps and price hurts.

The expensive chair on judgement — synthesis and the actual recommendation.

That turns "free models are weaker" into the reason to have them. Note the risk runs the other way too: small models are more agreeable, so free chairs need the sharpest personas, not the softest.

Panel or debate

Panel — parallel, each bot answers independently. Independent opinions, cheaper, faster.

Debate — sequential, each bot sees the previous answer. Actual argument, and N× the calls.

Different products. Worth choosing per session rather than defaulting into one.
